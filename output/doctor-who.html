Doctor Who: Sci vs Fi, season by season

Each season is scored on two independent axes. Sci is how much the episode text turns on technical and scientific vocabulary; Fi is how much it turns on god-like power, prophecy and reality-bending. Scores are computed per episode from a source scope, then averaged per season; bubble size is the measured 95% confidence interval of that average, never a hand-set value. Covers 3 series, 3 films and 22 seasons; 3 sources in scope (tvmaze + wikipedia + tardis) — toggle them on the right and the chart recomputes. Films have no TVmaze entry and are plotted with a neutral marker; their text comes from the same wiki sources, looked up by article title rather than search. Fi is a two-coder composite only where a franchise has a curated entity list (0 of 6 works); elsewhere it is lexicon-only, and the table says which. A source only contributes a score if it supplied at least 200 words of prose; the text basis column reads measured/covered·median words, so a source that resolved most episodes with a 30-word teaser is shown as covering them and measuring none of them. Scores are densities and densities bunch, so the sidebar can switch the chart to a percentile rank scale that uses the full range — read the measured scale for how much science a work contains, the rank scale for where it stands.

688 of 872 works in this franchise have no measurement at all under the full source scope: every source either missed them or supplied fewer than 200 words of prose. They are absent from the chart rather than plotted at zero, so an empty region here is a gap in the evidence, not a low score. Largest gaps: dw63 682, dw05 6. Deselecting a source can only increase this. The fix is better source text, not a different number.

Measured scores are densities, and densities bunch: half of all works sit under 2.5 of 10, so the top of a 0–10 axis is empty. The normalised view ranks each work within the corpus per source and plots that, which guarantees the full range and cancels the level differences between sources — but a rank says where a work sits, not how much science it contains. The baseline-v1 overlay is hidden here: those are hand-scored integers, and an integer 6 is not the same quantity as a rank of 6.

Off by default: the full 0–10 scale makes it plain how little of it the measured scores occupy. Zooming rescales the axes to the visible points, which exaggerates small gaps — read it alongside the confidence intervals, never on its own.

Rubric, uncertainty and provenance

Sci - scientific / engineering rigour

How much an episode's text turns on technical, scientific or engineering vocabulary, as opposed to being set dressing.

  • technical-lexicon-density — coder lexicon, 57 terms, weight 1.0, saturates at 15.019

Fi - myth / magic / reality-bending

How much an episode's text turns on god-like power, prophecy, magic-equivalent action or reality-breaking storytelling.

  • mythic-lexicon-density — coder lexicon, 59 terms, weight 1.0, saturates at 4.854
  • numinous-entity — coder entity, curated entity list (franchise:entities), weight 1.0, saturates at 23.654

per sub-signal: frac = min(1, value / saturation); axis score = max * sum(weight * frac) / sum(weight). Saturation is the 95th percentile of that sub-signal's raw value over all valid measurements in the repository (`calibrate`), so a score of 10 is the top 5% of what has actually been measured rather than an extrapolation. Raw densities are always published alongside scores, and each season also carries a percentile `rank` so the chart can be read on a scale that cannot bunch.

Saturation is calibrated, not asserted. p95 of the sub-signal's raw value over valid measurements in doctor-who, star-trek: technical-lexicon-density 20.0 → 15.019 (n=2016), mythic-lexicon-density 9.0 → 4.854 (n=2016), numinous-entity 6.0 → 23.654 (n=152). Regenerate with python3 -m sciorfi calibrate; a score is always a function of a named rubric file, never of the corpus it was read from.

Validity floor. Minimum source-text length for a measurement. Below it a source still counts as covering an episode (published in the text basis) but contributes no score. TVmaze summaries have a 33-word median and 92% of them contain no lexicon term at all.

Uncertainty. ci95-mean: 95% CI of the episode-level mean (z=1.96), inflated ×1.5 below 5 episodes. Measured from the episode distribution, never asserted. A season with a single measurement has no estimable interval: it is published as n/a and drawn large, not as ±0.00.

Bubble size is the 95% confidence interval of the season's episode-level mean — a measured quantity. Two things have no interval to encode and are drawn at a fixed size instead: a film (one work, not a season average) and a season measured on a single episode, which is drawn large and reported as n/a rather than ±0.00. Bubble position is the season score. Sci and Fi are scored from disjoint term sets, so they are independent by construction. On the normalised scale bubble position is a percentile rank and size is fixed, because a confidence interval has no meaning for a rank.
