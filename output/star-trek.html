Star Trek series: Sci vs Fi, season by season

Sci and Fi are scored independently against fixed rubrics, never as trade-offs: Sci is how much a season's plots hinge on solving an internally-consistent scientific/engineering problem; Fi is how much it leans on god-like power, prophecy, or reality-bending storytelling. A season can score high, low, or anything on both at once. Each blob's size is uncertainty, not data — bigger means less confidence, from a thin episode sample or from episode-to-episode swings within the season. The solid line traces a series' seasons in order; a dashed line connects the larger average marker to season 1, so it's easy to find where each series starts. A small jitter keeps seasons with identical or near-identical scores from stacking exactly on top of each other. Hover a blob for the season's exact scores, uncertainty, and the reasoning behind them.

Scoring criteria

Sci — scientific/engineering rigor

How much a season's plots hinge on solving an internally-consistent scientific or engineering problem, regardless of what else is happening.

  • 0–2 — science is set dressing only
  • 3–4 — occasional technical-problem episode among mostly non-technical plots
  • 5–6 — roughly a third to half of episodes turn on a scientific/engineering puzzle
  • 7–8 — most episodes are structured as a technical procedural
  • 9–10 — nearly every episode's core engine is rigorous problem-solving

Fi — myth, magic, reality-bending

How much a season features god-like beings, prophecy, magic-equivalent power, or reality/format-bending storytelling.

  • 0–2 — fully grounded, no mythic or reality-bending material
  • 3–4 — one isolated mythic entity or format-bender in an otherwise grounded season
  • 5–6 — a recurring mythic thread present in a minority of episodes
  • 7–8 — mythic/reality-bending material is a frequent, defining feature
  • 9–10 — myth or fantasy is the season's primary engine

Uncertainty (shown as blob size) comes from two independent sources per axis: thin data (short seasons, anthologies, a single film, or a season that aired after my reliable knowledge window) and within-season swings (one outlier episode against an otherwise consistent season). Sci and Fi carry their own uncertainty separately — a season can be confident on one axis and shaky on the other.

Notable outliers — seasons far from their own series' average

Discovery season 4 (Sci 8, Fi 4) vs. the rest of Discovery (~Sci 5, Fi 7)
Every other Discovery season is built around a techno-mystical macguffin (spore drive, Red Angel, Progenitor tech) treated with reverence rather than rigor. Season 4's DMA arc breaks that pattern: it's a single anomaly investigated methodically across the whole season, resolved through actual mathematics and first-contact protocol rather than a mystical reveal. It's the one Discovery season built like a classic hard-sci-fi procedural instead of a myth wearing a sci-fi costume — hence the jump to the show's highest Sci and lowest Fi, both with unusually low uncertainty.
Strange New Worlds season 4 (Sci 2, Fi 8) vs. seasons 1–3 (~Sci 6, Fi 6)
Seasons 1–3 kept a fairly steady "alien of the week" problem-solving format with occasional format experiments. Season 4 abandons that format itself — noir, puppets, hallucinations, a literal dinosaur apocalypse, a space baby — so the drop isn't one bad science episode, it's the season declining to be science-driven as a structural choice.
Picard season 2 (Sci 3, Fi 8) vs. seasons 1 and 3 (~Sci 5, Fi 6)
Q rewriting the timeline and stranding the crew in an alternate 2024 removes the show's usual technical throughline almost entirely for a season; seasons 1 and 3 both keep at least one technical threat (synth ethics, Borg-assimilated fleet) running alongside the melodrama.
DS9 season 7 (Sci 3, Fi 8) vs. seasons 1–2 (Sci 4, Fi 6)
This is a gradual drift rather than a single jump — the Dominion War pulls the show away from science steadily across seasons 5–7, and the finale's fulfillment of Sisko's prophecy as the Emissary is the endpoint of that drift, not an isolated spike.
