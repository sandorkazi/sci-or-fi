Star Trek: Sci vs Fi, season by season

Each season is scored on two independent axes. Sci is how much the episode text turns on technical and scientific vocabulary; Fi is how much it turns on god-like power, prophecy and reality-bending. Scores are computed per episode from a source scope, then averaged per season; bubble size is the measured 95% confidence interval of that average, never a hand-set value. Seasons of a series are joined in broadcast order, so each series is a path over the years: its first season is marked with its broadcast season number and the line fades in from there to the latest season. Covers 14 of 15 series, 14 films and 66 seasons; 3 sources in scope (tvmaze + wikipedia + memory-alpha) — toggle them on the right and the chart recomputes. Films have no TVmaze entry and are plotted with a neutral marker; their text comes from the same wiki sources, looked up by article title rather than search. Fi is a two-coder composite: a mythic lexicon plus a curated named-entity list, coded per series and per film (14 of 28 works have a list; the rest are lexicon-only, and the Fi coder column says which). The two are combined by max rather than a mean, because the entity list is a recovery coder — it exists to see gods that plot summaries name rather than describe — and averaging its many zeros would pull down the lexicon's hits. A source only contributes a score if it supplied at least 200 words of prose; the text basis column reads measured/covered·median words, so a source that resolved most episodes with a 30-word teaser is shown as covering them and measuring none of them. Scores are densities and densities bunch, so the sidebar can switch the chart to a percentile rank scale that uses the full range — read the measured scale for how much science a work contains, the rank scale for where it stands.

Each series is joined in broadcast order from its first season (marked with that season's number — a split era can start mid-run) to its latest, and the line fades in at the start so direction in time is visible. Turn paths off to compare seasons as a cloud; the tooltip still names the season.

Measured scores are densities, and densities bunch: half of all works sit under 2.5 of 10, so the top of a 0–10 axis is empty. The normalised view ranks each work within the corpus per source and plots that, which guarantees the full range and cancels the level differences between sources — but a rank says where a work sits, not how much science it contains. The baseline-v1 overlay is hidden here: those are hand-scored integers, and an integer 6 is not the same quantity as a rank of 6.

Off by default: the full 0–10 scale makes it plain how little of it the measured scores occupy. Fitting rescales the axes to the visible points, which exaggerates small gaps — read it alongside the confidence intervals, never on its own. Season scores

Rubric, uncertainty and provenance

Sci - scientific / engineering rigour

How much an episode's text turns on technical, scientific or engineering vocabulary, as opposed to being set dressing.

  • technical-lexicon-density — coder lexicon, 57 terms, weight 1.0, saturates at 14.472

Fi - myth / magic / reality-bending

How much an episode's text turns on god-like power, prophecy, magic-equivalent action or reality-breaking storytelling.

  • mythic-lexicon-density — coder lexicon, 59 terms, weight 1.0, saturates at 5.097
  • numinous-entity — coder entity, curated entity list (franchise:entities), weight 1.0, saturates at 12.285

This axis is combined by max, not by a weighted mean. The Fi axis is one dense coder plus one sparse recovery coder. The entity list exists because plot summaries name gods rather than describing them mythically, so it recovers myth that the lexicon cannot see; it is not a second opinion about episodes the lexicon already scores. Averaging would let its many zeros pull down the lexicon's hits, so the axis takes the strongest evidence either coder sees. Set combine to "weighted" to average them instead.

per sub-signal: frac = min(1, value / saturation); axis score = max * sum(weight * frac) / sum(weight). Saturation is the 95th percentile of that sub-signal's raw value over all valid measurements in the repository (`calibrate`), so a score of 10 is the top 5% of what has actually been measured rather than an extrapolation. Raw densities are always published alongside scores, and each season also carries a percentile `rank` so the chart can be read on a scale that cannot bunch.

Saturation is calibrated, not asserted. p95 of the sub-signal's raw value over valid measurements in doctor-who, star-trek: technical-lexicon-density 14.472 → 14.472 (n=2289), mythic-lexicon-density 5.097 → 5.097 (n=2289), numinous-entity 23.654 → 12.285 (n=2233). Regenerate with python3 -m sciorfi calibrate; a score is always a function of a named rubric file, never of the corpus it was read from.

Validity floor. Minimum source-text length for a measurement. Below it a source still counts as covering an episode (published in the text basis) but contributes no score. TVmaze summaries have a 33-word median and 92% of them contain no lexicon term at all.

Uncertainty. ci95-mean: 95% CI of the episode-level mean (z=1.96), inflated ×1.5 below 5 episodes. Measured from the episode distribution, never asserted. Where no interval is estimable — a season with one measurement, or one whose source set includes a source that resolved a single work — it is published as n/a and drawn large, not as ±0.00.

Bubble size is the 95% confidence interval of the season's episode-level mean — a measured quantity. Some things have no interval to encode and are drawn at a fixed size instead: a film (one work, not a season average), and a season with no estimable interval — either it rests on a single measurement, or one of its sources resolved only one work — which is drawn large and reported as n/a rather than ±0.00. Bubble position is the season score. Series with unit: serial were broadcast as parts and are analysed as serials, so their counts are stories, not transmission parts. Sci and Fi are scored from disjoint term sets, so they are independent by construction. On the normalised scale bubble position is a percentile rank and size is fixed, because a confidence interval has no meaning for a rank.
